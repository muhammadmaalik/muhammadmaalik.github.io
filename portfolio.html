Space systems / July 2026 - present

PsiSat

A 1U gamma-ray burst nanosatellite

Design study / proposal

A student CubeSat proposal to detect short flashes of high-energy light from orbit, with a compact aluminum shell and six planned electronics layers.

TOOLS Fusion 360, PCB design, detector integration

Read project notes

Purpose

A scintillator converts incoming gamma rays into light near 550 nm. A sensor reads each flash and a small computer records its timing.

My contribution

I started the project, recruited the team, and wrote the NASA CubeSat Launch Initiative proposal. The shell is modeled in Fusion 360. Jackson Boyle and Christian Tumanda are also on the proposal.

Packaging & status

The target envelope is about 10 x 10 x 11 cm, with mass below 1.33 kg. Six electronics layers are planned. Stage 4 is the power board and Stage 5 holds the batteries; four other boards remain in design. PsiSat has not launched.

Analysis / Summer internship, 2026

Vibration isolation

Crystal oscillator mounting study

Engineering study

A modal study of a 10 MHz crystal oscillator on four isolators, identifying the frequency ranges where its mounts amplify or reduce vibration.

TOOLS Ansys Mechanical, modal analysis

Read project notes

The assembly

At East End Assemblies, I studied a Wenzel 501-27521-11 oscillator mounted on a plate with four J-3112-2 isolators.

The analysis

I ran modal analysis in Ansys Mechanical and examined the first six natural frequencies. Low modes were near 30-50 Hz, where bench motion can be amplified. Isolation began to help above roughly 70 Hz; the mounts stiffened at higher frequencies.

Engineering purpose

The study described where the mounting system helps and where it does not before installation. These findings apply to the studied assembly and model.

Analysis / September 2025 - present

Rover structural analysis

Frame design, load cases & weight reduction

FEA lead / Stony Brook Robotics

Design and analysis of the URC rover frame, using static and impact cases to guide structural changes before fabrication.

TOOLS SolidWorks, Ansys Mechanical, LS-DYNA

Read project notes

My contribution

I design the URC rover frame in SolidWorks and evaluate it in Ansys Mechanical. LS-DYNA supports short impact cases. Successive load-case iterations reduced the rover mass by 6 kg.

Top-frame study

The September 2026 pass modeled aluminum 6061-T6 members with 5 mm joint connections. Loads of 100 N, 150 N, and 150 N were applied with selected faces fixed. This initial pass used the default mesh.

Design response

Peak deformation on the middle member pointed to the layout as the area to improve. Proposed changes included additional supports, filleted trusses or L-bars, and evaluation of 2 x 2 inch tubing.

Robotics / SBU Hacks 2026

Batman

Autonomous trash-pickup robot

Team prototype

A low-cost robot designed to find and collect campus litter, bringing computer vision, omnidirectional motion, and a two-servo claw into one hardware prototype.

TOOLS Arduino UNO Q, Ultralytics YOLO11, Python, mecanum drive, TCP / Wi-Fi

More build photos
Read project notes

The idea

Litter collects under benches, beside bins, and along campus walkways. Our team built Batman to explore a small, affordable robot that can reach those spots and pick it up.

See, decide, move, grab

An iPhone streams video to a Windows laptop running a trash-trained YOLO11 model on an RTX 5060. Drive and arm commands travel over TCP on a shared hotspot to the UNO Q. Four motors and two HW-166 (TB6612) drivers power the mecanum base; lift and gripper servos operate the claw.

Integration & lessons

We tested the motors and servos separately before integrating vision. Stable logic power and a common ground resolved driver issues. The stock Zephyr core lacked Servo.h, so the team used soft 50 Hz servo pulses. A shared hotspot replaced campus Wi-Fi for the robot link.

Current state & next steps

The physical base, arm controller, and GPU detector are built. Reliable pickup remains a tuning task: center on detections, improve the grab sequence, tidy the harness, and extend battery runs. Obstacle sensing and onboard compute are future options.

Analysis / June 2024

Hydrogen engine study

Comparative performance & operating constraints

Comparative study

An investigation of electrolysis-derived hydrogen in an internal combustion engine, comparing performance and operating demands with gasoline.

TOOLS Research, Python, data analysis

Read project notes

Research question

Compared engine behavior and operating overhead for hydrogen and gasoline, focusing on efficiency, speed, and thermal demands.

Reported observations

The study reported 40% higher RPM and 60% higher operating temperature for the hydrogen-fueled engine relative to the gasoline baseline. The thermal result motivated closer attention to material constraints.

Design implication

The temperature result highlights thermal management and material constraints as priorities for subsequent investigation.
